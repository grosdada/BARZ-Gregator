Prompt Skills Update 2026-10-03: Wan 3.0, MiniMax H3, FLUX 3, Kling 3

Companion to prompt-engines-reference.html (same date, same conventions). That file is unchanged. This file adds four complete, corrected engine skills. Load both.

0. How to use this update (instructions for any LLM)

  1. This document contains four engine skills. Each one is complete and self-contained: it replaces the earlier skill of the same name in full.
  2. When the user names one of these engines, follow the matching skill section here. Use prompt-engines-reference.html for cross-engine craft (directing, staging, photography, control techniques).
  3. Precedence: if a rule here differs from an older copy of a skill named wan30-prompt, minimax-h3-prompt, flux3-prompt or kling3-video-prompt, this document wins. It does not contradict the reference file.
  4. Each skill starts with a change log: old rule, new rule, source tag. Read it first if you hold an older version.

0.1 Output conventions shared by the four skills [P]

  • Deliver the prompt in an HTML widget with a copy-to-clipboard button. Plain code block as fallback.
  • Prompt body in English. Surrounding commentary in the user's language.
  • No em dashes in the prompt or the commentary.
  • Never format the prompt itself with markdown.
  • Below the prompt: one settings line (mode, duration, ratio, resolution, audio), then the reference load order, then the one or two shots most likely to fail with their fallback.
  • Never name a real person, a brand or a protected character in a prompt. Describe features.
  • Never name a fabrication technique (latex, puppet, prosthetic, miniature, CGI). Describe the visible result.

1.3 Prompt architecture [O]

[Overall description: format, genre, one sentence of intent.]
[Reference citations with roles: "the face and build of Image 1", "the set dressing and palette of Image 2", "camera behaving as in Video 1".]

Shot 1 [0-3s]: [subject] + [scene] + [motion] + [aesthetics: shot size, angle, light] + [dialogue] + [sound or BGM] + [style and mood].
Shot 2 [3-7s]: hard cut. [the same man in the ochre jacket] + ...
Shot 3 [7-10s]: ...

No subtitles. No watermark. No slow motion.
  • Accepted shot markers: Shot 1 [0-3s], Segment 1 (00:00-00:03), [00:00-00:03]. Segments are contiguous. 2 to 5 seconds per segment.
  • Subject lock in the first 25 words: age, build, face, hair, wardrobe with materials and colours. Restate the subject in one clause after every cut. [O] [P]
  • Name light sources, shot size and angle. Short vague prompts are auto-cut into several stock-like shots. [3P]
  • Put style as an intent sentence at the top, not as trailing adjectives ("cinematic, 8k, masterpiece" is noise). [P]
  • Turn prompt_extend off (or "Auto Polish" on hosts) whenever the prompt has shot labels and timestamps. [3P]

One-take variant [O] [P]

One continuous shot, no cuts.
[Subject lock in 25 words.]
The camera opens on [station A], holds two seconds, then [one move] toward [station B] as [action]. It settles on [station C] when [end state].
[Environment and light.] [Audio.]
No subtitles. No watermark.

Write "One continuous shot" or "Generate single shot" on the first line. For takes over 10 seconds, write the camera as one unbroken path with named stations. Use a fixed duration when the clip must fit an edit; use -1 when the beats are written but pacing is uncertain.

1.4 References [O]

  • Token: Image 1, Video 2, Audio 1. No @. Images, videos and audio are numbered separately, each in upload order. Numbers must match upload order.
  • A single material may be called "Reference image" or "Reference video".
  • Always cite by role. A bare "Image 1" leaves the model to guess what to copy.
  • The same material can be cited several times for different roles.
  • When a reference audio drives lip-sync, do not also write the line as text. [P]
  • OpenArt uses @Image1 on its own interface. [3P]

1.6 Editing and extension [O]

Edit the video: replace [X] in Video 1 with [Y], keep the rest unchanged.
Edit the video: at 4-6s, change [X] to [Y]. Keep everything else unchanged.
Edit the video: replace the line at 2-5s with: "[new line]", keep the same voice timbre.
Extend Video 1 forward. [Restate character and scene details.] [New action.]

Change one thing per edit. Extension keywords: extend, continue, with a direction (forward, backward, both).

1.7 Exclusions

  • Official: a short negative list at the very end, not repeating positive content: "No subtitles. No watermark. No slow motion." [O]
  • Production: for a critical exclusion, also state the wanted state positively inside the shot ("the laptop lid is bare brushed aluminium, unmarked"). [P]
  • Vocabulary: write "static" or "locked off", not "tripod". [P]

1.8 Failure modes

  • Vendor-admitted: audio texture, exact on-screen text, word-exact lip-sync, very long action sequences, rapid scene changes. [O]
  • High-frequency actions it cannot control: avoid. [O]
  • Prompt enhancer rewriting labels and timestamps: turn it off. [3P]
  • Independent adherence beyond 20 seconds is still thinly tested. Say so. [P]

1.10 Checklist

  1. Mode chosen; frame control not mixed with references.
  2. Duration 2 to 30 or -1; input plus output within 30 s for extension.
  3. Subject locked in the first 25 words and restated after each cut.
  4. Shots contiguous, 2 to 5 s each, timestamps sum to the duration.
  5. References cited as Image N with a role, numbers matching upload order.
  6. Dialogue in the official form; "No dialogue" or "No BGM" written when wanted.
  7. Negative list at the end; critical exclusions also stated positively.
  8. prompt_extend off for structured prompts.

2.1 Specs [O]

  • Endpoint POST /v2/video_generation, asynchronous. Open weights announced 2026-08-03 (the Context-IR preprocessor and the 2K regeneration module are API-only).
  • Duration 4 to 15 seconds, integers only (H3-Max 5 to 15). 24 fps. Stereo audio, 32 kHz.
  • Resolution 768P or 2K. Ratios: adaptive, 21:9, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16.
  • Prompt up to 7,000 characters. Reference-mode description target: 350 to 500 words.
  • References: 9 images, 3 videos (2 to 15 s each, 15 s total), 3 audio (15 s total), 12 files, request up to 64 MB. Audio cannot be sent alone.
  • Languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish.
  • No negative-prompt field. No seed continuity between 768P and 2K. [O/3P]

2.4 Simple block format [P]

Valid on hosted H3, where the platform expander rewrites it into the native format. On raw open weights, use 2.3.

Use Image 1 <Picture 1> as the locked design reference for [subject]. Preserve exactly: [3 to 8 hard traits: silhouette, part count, colour blocking, markings, wear]. The geometry stays rigid and identical in every shot. No morphing, no scale drift.
Match the camera movement from Video 1 <Video 1>. Motion only, not the subject, not the location, not the palette.

Style: [shooting situation: medium, era, who films and how], [light], [palette], natural grain. [Global rule for every shot.]

Shot 1 - 3 seconds. Camera: 180mm telephoto, locked off, ground level, static. [One action.]
Shot 2 - 2 seconds. Camera: 24mm, low, pushes in with large amplitude at fast speed. [One action.]
Shot 3 - 2 seconds. Camera: 50mm, handheld, pans right with small amplitude at slow speed. [One action.]

Audio, native stereo: NO MUSIC, none at any point. Diegetic sound only: [4 to 8 sounds tied to the shots, in order]. No voice, no dialogue.

Settings: 21:9, 2K, 7 seconds.
  • Durations are whole seconds and sum exactly to the total. Practical floor 2 seconds. Give 3 seconds to the shot carrying the most information.
  • For 15 seconds: 3x5 (contemplative), 5x3 (documentary standard), 6x2 plus 1x3 (reportage). More than 7 shots stops reading as cuts.
  • One action per shot. Vary lens, support and movement between consecutive shots.
  • Focal lengths work. F-stops, ISO and frame rates are ignored.
  • Put the music prohibition first in the audio line.
  • State what to ignore in each reference, not only what to take.

2.5 Editing and replacement [P]

[video editing]
Replace only [who, identified by what they are in the plate] in <Video 1> with the character in <Picture 1>.
attribute_transfer from <Picture 1>: face, hair, outfit, body proportions, held for the whole clip.
<Video 1> is fully_preserved for the background, the props, the camera path, framing, focus, motion blur, lighting, shadows, grain and the original edit.
Maintain correct occlusion. Match scale and contact shadows. Add no camera movement of your own. Do not add new movement, objects, cuts, text or accessories.
  • Lead with a trigger verb (replace, remove, relight, restyle). Name the camera path, motion blur and grain in the preservation clause.
  • Match the reference framing to the plate framing (full body to full body).
  • Two edit passes at most: décor first, then cast.

2.7 Checklist

  1. Mode stated. Frame slots and reference slots not both filled.
  2. Duration is an integer from 4 to 15; shot durations or timecodes consistent with it.
  3. Every reference cited by number with what to take and what to ignore.
  4. No angle brackets other than reference and dialogue tags.
  5. Camera moves carry amplitude and speed; one move per shot.
  6. Audio directed; music explicitly on or off.
  7. Exclusions written inline as constraint sentences.

3.1 Specs [O]

  • Endpoint /v1/flux-3-video. Preview model. Duration 5 to 20 seconds in whole seconds, or auto. Continuation 5 to 15 seconds.
  • 24 fps: frame index = seconds x 24.
  • Ratios: auto, 21:9, 2:1, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16, 9:21.
  • Audio on by default: multilingual speech with lip-sync, effects, ambience.
  • Keyframes: 1 image is the start frame, 2 are start and end, up to 10 timestamped. Positions by frame or by timestamp, all unique, none beyond the duration.
  • Keyframes and continuation cannot share a request. [P]

  • No prompt limit on BFL; 10,000 characters on Runware [3P]. "Grounding" toggle on fal, on by default [3P].
  • The model rewrites and expands the prompt: stated layers are respected, open layers are invented. [3P]

Draft is a cheap first pass on text-to-video, not a mode. Fix every prompt problem at draft stage: enhance adds detail on the same seed and motion, it does not re-plan. [O] [P]

3.3 Prompt shapes [O]

  1. Short phrase: look development only.
  2. One-liner (default): camera, subject, action, environment, supporting motion, look and sound, in one flowing sentence.
  3. Labelled lines: camera / subject and action / depth of field / lighting / motion / audio / style. Best for changing one line per pass.
  4. Timestep beats: 0.0-3.5s: ... with HARD CUT. where a cut belongs.

Complex sequences: core summary, scene, subject description, dynamic narrative (timecoded), audio, style and colour. Five elements to cover in any shape: subject and action, camera direction, scene and atmosphere, motion qualities, continuity constraints.

  • Plain prose, concrete nouns and verbs. One framing term, one movement term, one clear action.
  • About 5 to 7 seconds per beat. Each beat: starting state, one event, observable end state. [O] [P]
  • Write the prompt body in English; quote dialogue in the language spoken. Check translation traps ("football", "chips", "veste"). [P]
  • Naming a format or an era carries camera, light and editing grammar for free: "a 1987 local news report". [P]

3.4 Camera [O] [P]

  • Reliable moves: static or locked off, dolly or push in, pull back, pan or tilt, orbit, handheld tracking. Also documented: crane, whip pan, dolly zoom, Steadicam follow, snorricam, roll, arc, pedestal, trucking.
  • Say the move and its duration: "camera pushes slowly forward from wide to close over six seconds".
  • The default is a slow drift. For no movement write "camera locked at eye level".
  • Lens character: "35mm, shallow depth of field", "70mm long lens, compressed background", "anamorphic, oval bokeh". Focus terms: rack focus, split diopter, tilt shift, deep focus.

3.5 Audio and dialogue [O]

  • Four layers: speech, ambience (named sources), effects (tied to visible actions), music (style, pace, mix position). The layer named first sits in front. [P]
  • Speech: visible speaker + delivery + exact words in double quotes. Voiceover must be labelled "off-screen voiceover" or "narration".
  • Voice direction: person (age, accent), register, recording perspective, delivery, guardrails ("no announcer delivery").
  • Label the language per line; write "same voice" across a language switch.
  • About ten words per four seconds. Allow 1 to 2 seconds before speech starts.
  • Always add no on-screen text, no subtitles with dialogue.
  • Do not ask for "silence": name the room tone, or write "No music. Room tone only."
  • Effects as surface plus action: "boots on wet cobblestone". Abstract cues render thin. [P]

3.6 Multi-shot [O]

SHOT ONE: [framing and action]. HARD CUT.
SHOT TWO: [new framing, angle or location]. HARD CUT.
SHOT THREE: [closing beat].
Music: [one bed for the whole sequence]. no on-screen text, no subtitles.
  • HARD CUT. in capitals with a full stop. Two or three shots per generation.
  • Adjacent shots must differ strongly in scale, angle, location or light.
  • Cut on completed motion. Name the music bed once; name ambience per shot. [P]
  • One take: "one continuous unbroken shot".
  • Longer films: one generation per shot, an identical world-bible paragraph pasted verbatim, the same musical motif named each time.

3.7 FLUX Video Edit [O]

  • Separate endpoint (/v1/flux-tools/video-edit-v1). Operations: add, remove, replace, restyle, change dialogue.
  • Source up to 15 seconds and 50 MiB, downscaled to 720p. Prompt 1 to 4,096 characters.
  • No image, video or audio reference. No masks. No extension.
  • Write one instruction: the target, the change, what stays.

3.8 Failure modes [O/3P]

  • Over-stuffed prompts reduce motion coherence: tighten.
  • Complex multi-person coordinated action is hard; kinetic action is weaker than dialogue scenes.
  • Multi-image storyboards struggle with large motion between keyframes.
  • In-prompt exclusions help but are not guaranteed.
  • Per-shot timings live in the prose only: uneven splits are a steer, not a guarantee. [P]

3.9 FLUX 3 Image [O]

  • Generation and editing in one endpoint. Up to 10 references (256x256 minimum, 16 MP maximum each).
  • Sizes 768sq, 1k (default), 2k, 4k. Ratios: 21:9, 2:1, 16:9, 3:2, 7:5, 4:3, 5:4, 1:1, 4:5, 3:4, 5:7, 2:3, 9:16, 1:2, 9:21, plus auto (follows the first reference).
  • No seed, width or upsampling field. The expanded prompt is returned.
  • No negative prompt: rewrite positively.
[Image type or medium], [subject, specifically described], [action or pose], in [setting], [light: source, direction, quality, what it does], [framing and detail].
  • "Put first what matters most." For realism, describe what a camera records. One or two effects at most. No quality tags.
  • References by position with roles: "image 1 is the subject, image 2 is the setting, image 3 is the style". Say how they relate.
  • Edit: target, change, what stays: "Modify <ref_image_0> by replacing ... with ... Keep the rest unchanged."
  • Text: exact words in quotation marks, placement, typography; \n for line breaks. Hero typography still belongs in post. [O] [P]
  • Hex colour: #RRGGBB preceded by "color" or "hex", tied to one named object.
  • Layout: caption cites element ids as <id> with JSON rows (id, bbox, desc) on a 0 to 1000 grid. Coordinate order is reported inconsistently: test before relying on it. [U]
  • FLUX.2 remains the route when open weights, seeds or JSON prompts are needed.

3.10 Checklist

  1. Video or image decided first. Mode legal for the assets.
  2. Duration 5 to 20 (5 to 15 for continuation), matched to the number of beats.
  3. Camera named with a duration, or explicitly locked.
  4. Every audio layer named; dialogue quoted with a visible speaker; subtitles refused.
  5. Multi-shot: HARD CUT., contrasted shots, three at most, music named once.
  6. Keyframes: ten at most, unique positions, frame maths shown.
  7. No promise of reference-to-video.

4.2 Prompt anatomy

Order: scene and ambience, characters with fixed unique names, action and dialogue, camera, progression. [3P]. Cover seven elements [P]:

  1. Subject: identity, visual traits, wardrobe, expression, props. In the first sentence.
  2. Environment: location, weather, time of day.
  3. Lighting: source, direction, quality, colour temperature.
  4. Camera and framing: shot size, angle, lens feel, one movement.
  5. Motion and physics: one action, its speed and energy in words ("barely moves", "walks slowly", "sprints"), weight and contact.
  6. Style and mood: realism level, grade, texture. No mixed incompatible styles.
  7. Audio: dialogue, ambience, foley, or silence.
  • Unique consistent character labels. No pronouns. Bind every line to a speaker and an action. [3P]
  • Describe subject behaviour and camera behaviour separately. [3P]
  • Do not compress a narrative into one paragraph: use shots. [3P]
  • Image-to-video: describe what moves and how the camera moves; do not re-describe the image. [3P]

4.3 Formats

Single shot

[Shot size] of [named subject with traits] in [environment]. [One action, with speed and energy in words.] Camera: [one movement], [angle], [framing progression]. Lighting: [source, direction, quality]. Style: [look, grade, mood]. Audio: [ambience, foley]. Mara (quietly, tired): "[line]"

Custom multi-shot (up to 6 shots, total 15 s or less)

Shot 1 (4s): [shot size], [named subject + environment + one action], [camera movement], [lighting and style].
Shot 2 (3s): [shot size], [same named subject, new action], [camera movement].
Shot 3 (5s): [shot size], [resolution], [camera movement]. Mara (softly, surprised): "[line]"
  • Each shot: number, duration, framing, one action, camera movement. Durations stated and summed.
  • On the API, send each shot as one entry of multi_prompt with its own duration.
  • Official Omni storyboard examples use time-range blocks per shot with shot type, action, dialogue and camera. [O]

Dialogue [O] [3P]

Official: Mom (softly, surprised): "You're home early."
Host variant (fal): [Mara: woman in her thirties, low calm voice]: "You're home early." Immediately, [Jon: tired, hoarse]: "I never left."

"Immediately," and "Pause," pace the turns. On fal, languages other than Chinese and English may be translated to English [3P].

4.5 Omni patterns [O]

Motion transfer: Animate the character in @Image1 with the same motion as the character in @Video1.
Camera transfer: Follow the camera movement of @Video1. [New subject and scene.]
Start and end: Take @Image1 as the start frame and @Image2 as the end frame. [Describe the change.]
Instruction edit: In @Video1, replace [X] with [the object in @Image1]. Keep everything else unchanged.
  • With a reference video: at most 4 images or elements, and no new audio generation.
  • A multi-angle element holds identity better than a single image: one image forces the model to guess unseen sides.
  • Stable labels for several characters; three or more are supported.

4.6 Motion Control [O]

  • Inputs: a character image plus a motion video (3 to 30 s) or a library motion.
  • "Matches Video": movement, expression, camera and orientation follow the reference. "Matches Image": motion follows the video, orientation follows the image, camera can be prompted.
  • The prompt controls background and scene.
  • Requirements: one character; body and head unobstructed; steady moderate motion; no cuts or camera moves in the driving video; framing matched (half body to half body, full to full).

4.7 Negative prompt (API hosts only) [P]

General: temporal flicker, warping, morphing, unstable anatomy, floating objects, inconsistent physics, background drift, color banding
Humans: deformed hands, incorrect finger count, asymmetrical face, unnatural joints, distorted eyes, rubber limbs, broken walking cycle, identity drift
Products and text: logo distortion, text deformation, brand color shift, warped product geometry, unreadable labels
Continuity: jump cuts inside a shot, inconsistent lighting direction, scale changes, object disappearance, unnatural occlusion

Tailor it to the subject. Where no negative field exists (web app, MCP, Omni), fold the two or three critical exclusions into the prompt as positive states: "hands relaxed, five fingers each, steady identity". Kling's own guidance suggests "morphing features" and "changing clothes" as secondary negatives [O].

4.8 Failure modes [3P]

  • Quality drops in the last seconds of 15 second clips: keep the key beat early, or cut shorter.
  • Fine hand control, exact counts, complex physics are weak.
  • Motion control dislikes fast or chaotic motion.
  • Contradictory motion in one shot (slow and frantic): split into two shots. [P]
